How we build a piece

Every Northwind piece starts as rough boards that rest in the workshop for two weeks, so the wood settles to the room before we cut it.

Joints are cut by hand and glued with a water-based adhesive. We do not use metal fasteners where wood can hold the load.

Surfaces get three coats of hard wax oil. Each coat cures overnight and is buffed by hand the next morning.

Before a piece leaves, two people check it: one for the finish, one for stability on an uneven floor.

Delivery is by our own van within 80 km of the studio. Further away, a partner courier carries the piece in a padded crate.

If something arrives damaged, send a photo within seven days and we repair or replace it at no cost.

Spare felt pads, a tin of wax and a care card come with every order.

We keep the offcuts from your piece. Ask for them and we turn them into a matching set of coasters.

Oiling Water marks Sunlight Felt pads Moving furniture

Oiling

Wipe the surface clean, then rub in a thin coat of hard wax oil along the grain.

Leave it for fifteen minutes and buff away whatever the wood did not take in.

Use a lint-free cotton cloth. Kitchen paper leaves fibres in the grain that show once the oil cures.

Work in a room above 15 °C so the oil flows into the pores instead of sitting on top.

Two thin coats a year are enough for a dining table. Desks and side tables need one.

Water marks

A fresh ring usually lifts with a warm, dry cloth and gentle pressure.

Older marks need a light pass with fine steel wool and a fresh coat of oil.

Never leave a wet glass on the wood overnight: the finish holds for hours, not for days.

On oak, black rings mean the water reached the tannin. Call us before you sand those.

Coasters made from the offcuts of your piece are free if you ask when you order.

Sunlight

Walnut lightens and oak darkens a little in strong sun. Both are normal.

Turn tabletops a quarter turn every few months so they change evenly.

The first three months change the colour most. After a year the change is hard to see.

Keep objects off a new tabletop for the first weeks, or their outline stays lighter.

Curtains or a light blind are enough protection in south-facing rooms.

Felt pads

Check the pads under chairs twice a year and replace worn ones.

Spare pads come with every order; more are free on request.

Hard floors scratch fastest under chairs that are pulled out several times a day.

Clean the pads with a dry brush: grit trapped in the felt acts like sandpaper.

Screw-in glides work better than adhesive pads on chairs with thin legs.

Moving furniture

Lift pieces instead of pushing them, and carry tables by the frame.

For a move across town we can wrap and carry the large pieces for you.

Take the legs off large tables before a move; each one comes off with four bolts.

Never stack chairs seat to seat without a blanket between them.

After a move, check that tables still stand level and adjust the glides if needed.
